# DMZ Tour — Which One to Book

**Purpose of this file:** the DMZ day (**Day 3 / Aug 13**) rides on one booked guided tour. This page compares the two tour styles worth booking, settles the minimum-age question for our kids (9, 11, 15), and lists the practical must-knows. Book through an authorized operator (Klook, KKday, Viator/GetYourGuide, or VIPTravel); the day itself is pickup → drop-off, no logistics on us.

> **TL;DR** — Two headline choices. **Option A** adds a 30-min **North Korean defector Q&A** (the thing reviewers rave about) for a full ~9-hour day. **Option B** is the **standard tour without the Q&A** — same headline sights (3rd Tunnel + Dora Observatory), cheaper, and available as a shorter **half-day**. If we're lukewarm on the Q&A, **Option B is the better-value pick** and the half-day frees the afternoon for the War Memorial cluster.
> **All three kids clear the age rule** for the standard tour — the 12+ limit people cite is JSA-only, and **JSA is closed anyway.** Everyone needs a **physical passport** on the day.

Every *sight* (tunnel + observatory are why people go) is identical between the two. So the decision is simply: **is the 30-min Q&A worth a full 9-hour day and the price bump?** If yes → A. If we'd rather a tighter morning and get to the War Memorial + National Museum sooner → **B (half-day)**.

## Minimum age — resolved

- **Standard DMZ tour (3rd Tunnel + Dora Observatory): no hard minimum.** Commonly stated as **7+**; under-36-months ride free. **Our 9, 11 and 15-year-olds are all fine.**
- The **12+** rule that shows up online is **JSA / Panmunjom only** — and the JSA is **closed to general tourists** (see below), so it never applies to what's bookable.
- **Physical caveat, not an age rule:** the 3rd Tunnel is a **long, steep, narrow descent** (~73 m down). Fine for our kids, but anyone claustrophobic can wait at the visitor center; a **monorail** covers the climb for those who'd rather not walk it.

## Practical must-knows

- **Physical passports for all 6** — digital copies / phone photos are **never** accepted at the military checkpoint. Passport numbers are usually needed **~2–3 weeks ahead** at booking.
- **Dress code:** no ripped jeans, sleeveless tops, mini-skirts or flip-flops. Closed-toe shoes; long-ish shorts/trousers are safe.
- **Closed Mondays:** DMZ sites run **Tue–Sun**. **Aug 13 is a Thursday — we're fine.**
- **Go in the morning** to beat crowds (the standard half-day is a morning slot anyway).
- **Book early:** August is peak season; the top-rated operators sell out.

---

## Why no JSA / Panmunjom

The **Joint Security Area** (the blue conference huts on the actual border) has been **closed to general tourists since the July 2023 border-crossing incident**, with no reopening date announced as of 2026. The JSA *visitor center* partially reopened in 2025, but the blue buildings themselves remain off-limits. Listings that advertise "JSA" now mean a **museum simulation** near Imjingak — **don't pay a JSA premium** (those tours run ₩150,000–195,000) for something that no longer includes the real border zone.

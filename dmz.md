# DMZ Tour — Which One to Book

**Purpose of this file:** the DMZ day (**Day 3 / Aug 13**) rides on one booked guided tour. This page compares the two tour styles worth booking, settles the minimum-age question for our kids (9, 11, 15), and lists the practical must-knows. Book through an authorized operator (Klook, KKday, Viator/GetYourGuide, VIP Travel, Trazy, or Cosmojin — Koridoor/USO exists but is JSA-oriented and pricier, skip it for a standard tour); the day itself is pickup → drop-off, no logistics on us.

> **TL;DR** — Two headline choices. **Option A** adds a 30-min **North Korean defector Q&A** (the thing reviewers rave about) for a full ~9-hour day. **Option B** is the **standard tour without the Q&A** — same headline sights (3rd Tunnel + Dora Observatory), cheaper, and available as a shorter **half-day**. If we're lukewarm on the Q&A, **Option B (half-day) is the better-value pick** and it frees the afternoon for the War Memorial cluster.
> **All three kids clear the age rule** for the standard tour — the 10–12 limit people cite is JSA-only, and **JSA is effectively closed** (see below). Everyone needs a **physical passport** on the day.

> **Half-day = core sights** (Imjingak + 3rd Tunnel + Dora Observatory, **usually** + Dorasan Station — but some half-days drop Dorasan, so confirm the stop list). The Gamaksan suspension bridge and Unification Village are **full-day-only** add-ons; a half-day (Option B, our pick) does **not** include them.

**Which to pick?** Option A's differentiator is purely the defector conversation — reviewers consistently call it the moment that turns the day from sightseeing into first-hand oral history. Every *sight* (tunnel + observatory are why people go) is identical between the two. So the decision is simply: **is the 30-min Q&A worth a full 9-hour day and the price bump?** If yes → A. If we'd rather a tighter morning and get to the War Memorial + National Museum sooner → **B (half-day)**.

## Minimum age — resolved

- **Standard DMZ tour (3rd Tunnel + Dora Observatory): minimum age varies by operator, commonly 7+** (under-36-months ride free; a few listings accept any age). It is **not** a universal rule — **confirm the minimum on the specific listing before booking.** Either way, our **9, 11 and 15-year-olds clear any standard minimum.**
- The **10–12 minimum** that shows up online is **JSA / Panmunjom only** (it varies by operator) — and the JSA is **effectively closed to general tourists** (see below), so it never applies to what's reliably bookable.
- **Physical caveat, not an age rule:** the 3rd Tunnel is a **350 m, ~11° ramp descending 73 m (~25 storeys), hunched under a 2 m ceiling** in stale, humid air — hard hats required. The **climb back up in August heat is the hard part**, not the descent: fine for fit kids but **pace the 9-year-old**. Anyone claustrophobic can wait at the visitor center; a **monorail** covers the climb — but it's **not offered on every tour and can cost extra / sell out**, so confirm it if you want it.
- **Before paying, confirm the 9-year-old is accepted on THIS listing.** Most standard tours are 7+ or no-limit, but at least one operator applies an **under-11 bar** without labeling it JSA — get it in writing so the 9-year-old isn't refused at check-in with no refund.
- **Nationality:** the **standard** DMZ tour we're booking is open to **almost every nationality** — no check needed for the vast majority. The restricted-nationality list (~50 countries, incl. China/Russia/India and much of the Middle East & Central Asia) is a **JSA-only** requirement and doesn't apply to the standard 3rd-Tunnel/Dora tour. Only confirm with the operator if someone holds a passport North Korea deems hostile.

## Practical must-knows

- **Physical passports for all 6** — digital copies / phone photos and foreigner-registration cards are **never** accepted at the military checkpoint (IDs are checked more than once). For a **standard** tour, operators typically need passport **numbers ~5–7 days ahead** (the ~2–3-week figure floating around is a **JSA** requirement); still, **book ~2 weeks out for August availability.** Pickup is ~7:30 from a **downtown meeting point (not the DDP hotel)**, so a passport left behind can't be retrieved in time — **lay out all 6 the night before.**
- **Dress code:** no ripped jeans, sleeveless/midriff tops, above-the-knee shorts/mini-skirts, or flip-flops/open-toed shoes; **no military-pattern clothing or political/graphic slogans**. Closed-toe shoes; long-ish shorts/trousers are safe. (Enforcement is strictest on JSA tours; standard tours are lighter but publish the same rules.)
- **Closed Mondays and Korean national holidays — but weekend holidays are excluded.** The 3rd Tunnel, Dora Observatory and Unification Village run **Tue–Sun** and close on national holidays **that fall on a weekday**; a holiday landing on a **Saturday or Sunday does NOT close them** (only Seollal & Chuseok close on weekends). So **Sat Aug 15 (Liberation Day) the sites are OPEN** (a normal Saturday); the substitute holiday on **Mon Aug 17 is moot** — Mondays are already closed. **Our date, Thu Aug 13, is a normal open day.**
- **Weather contingency (Aug = peak monsoon/typhoon).** Rain/haze can turn Dora Observatory's North-Korea view into a gray wall, and operators **do cancel for weather — sometimes without refund.** Book one with a **stated bad-weather refund/reschedule policy and read the cancellation terms before paying.** This is a single-booked linchpin day, so keep the **indoor War Memorial galleries as the rain-proof anchor** (its outdoor tanks/planes are weather-exposed).
- **Bring:** physical passports, **cash in KRW** (monorail/gondola/Imjingak vendors are often cash-preferred), and **water + hats + sunscreen** for a hot outdoor morning; closed-toe shoes. Guides on English tours increasingly expect a small tip (optional in Korea, but common on these).
- **Photography is restricted** at several stops: Dora Observatory has a **yellow photo line** (shoot only from behind it — a 2026 report suggests it's sometimes relaxed, but assume enforced) and the tunnel interior is a **no-photography / phones-in-lockers** zone. Brief the kids so nobody's device is confiscated.
- **Shopping is a per-listing property, not a tour-type guarantee.** Cheaper DMZ tours (~₩45,000–50,000) offset the price with a mandatory 45–60 min ginseng/amethyst/duty-free stop; pay ~₩10–15k more for a listing whose title explicitly says **"No Forced Shopping" / "without shopping stop."** Operators that market no-shopping DMZ tours: **VIP Travel** (vviptravel.com) and **KKday** (dedicated listing); Viator/GetYourGuide also carry "without shopping stop" listings. **Verify on the exact listing.**
- **Watch for add-on costs** not always in the headline price: **3rd-Tunnel monorail ₩12,200** (vs **₩9,200 walk-in** — a ~₩3,000 premium), and on some tours the **Imjingak Peace Gondola (~₩12,000–15,000 adult by cabin type / ~₩10,000 child, round trip)**.
- **Go in the morning** to beat crowds (the standard half-day is a morning slot anyway).
- **Book a couple of weeks ahead.** August can be busy and popular slots do fill — book ~2 weeks out to be safe for a party of 6. (Caveat: July–August is monsoon season, which *dampens* demand on rainy/low-visibility days, so it's not a guaranteed sellout. As of **late July 2026, Aug 13 is ~19 days out — inside a sensible booking window, so book soon.**)

## Why no JSA / Panmunjom

The **Joint Security Area** (the blue conference huts on the actual border) has been **closed to general tourists since the July 2023 border-crossing incident**, and the UN Command has since **permanently redesigned** the orientation tour to remove the blue-building crossing — so even a future reopening won't restore "step into North Korea." There's **no announced reopening date as of 2026** and civilian access is unreliable. The JSA *visitor center* (Camp Bonifas area) partially reopened in 2025, but Freedom House and the blue conference rooms remain off-limits.

⚠️ **You will still find live, bookable "JSA / Panmunjom" listings** — in three flavors: **(1) real permission-based tours** (e.g. VIP Travel, ~₩195,000) that only open dates the UN Command grants ~1–2 months ahead, sell out fast, need passports + ~7-day advance booking, and carry a **100% cancellation fee inside 7 days** with same-day cancellation always possible; **(2) standard DMZ tours wearing a "JSA" name** with a *speculative* attempt bolted on — if the JSA is closed that day (usually), you just do the DMZ portion, sometimes after paying the premium; and **(3) a "JSA Experience Museum" replica** in Unification Village (not the real border). **Assume the blue buildings are NOT included; read each listing's fine print before paying a JSA premium (~₩150,000–195,000).** For a guaranteed, reliable visit, book a **standard DMZ half-day** (3rd Tunnel + Dora Observatory) instead.

## Booking

- Checklist line tracks this: **"DMZ Tour (Aug 13) — book through an authorized operator (Klook, KKday, Viator/GetYourGuide, VIP Travel, Trazy, or Cosmojin). ~50,000–75,000 KRW/person (half-day). Bring passports."**
- Provide **all six passport numbers** at booking (standard tour: usually ~5–7 days ahead; book ~2 weeks earlier for August availability). **Confirm 6 can be seated together on one shared bus** — and if that's not confirmable in writing, **book a private/family DMZ tour** (at 6 pax the price gap is small). Watch age-tier pricing: group tours often charge kids near the adult rate.
- Reconfirm the **pickup point** — many operators use a downtown meeting spot (Hongdae / City Hall / Myeongdong), **not** the hotel door. The half-day returns **~13:30–14:00** to a central drop-off (a few operators run later, ~15:00 or beyond depending on traffic/checkpoint timing); allow 20–40 min transit onward to the War Memorial (see Day 3).
